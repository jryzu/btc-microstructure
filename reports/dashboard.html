Does order-book imbalance survive the fee?

No for takers — the signal is real, beats the spread, and is statistically strong out-of-sample, but its ~0.5 bps edge is ~40× smaller than the standard taker fee. Yes, defensively, for makers — it prices the adverse selection that quoting desks should be skewing against.

Finding 01 · the signal

Imbalance → forward return is cleanly monotone

Sorting observations into deciles of order-book imbalance, average forward mid returns rise monotonically from −0.34 bps (heavily ask-sided book) to +0.30 bps (heavily bid-sided) at 1 s, and from −0.83 to +0.65 bps at 5 s. Error bars are 95% intervals.

Finding 02 · microprice & regimes

Microprice is imbalance in disguise; the effect holds in both vol regimes

Algebraically, microprice − mid = (spread/2) × level-1 imbalance — and with the spread pinned at one tick, the two are the same signal up to scale. The imbalance–return relation survives splitting the sample at median 30s realized volatility, steepening slightly at the extremes when volatility is high.

Finding 03 · out-of-sample behavior

Predictions are calibrated, and the signal decays fast

On the final ~27 minutes (never touched during training or threshold selection), predicted P(up) tracks realized frequency near the diagonal at both horizons. The linear model’s weights concentrate on level-1/5 imbalance; per unit time the 1s edge is far stronger than the 5s edge.

Finding 04 · the economics

Gross edge, net ruin: the fee is the whole story

The execution test takes strong signals (threshold = 98th percentile of |signal|, chosen on validation only), buys the prevailing ask after 100 ms simulated latency, and exits at the bid after the horizon — one position at a time, fees charged both legs. Net P&L is linear in fee and crosses zero near 0.26 bps/side; latency alone kills the edge by ~1 s.

Reality check

Where the frictions actually bind

spread

One tick ($0.01 on ~$79,000) in 99.9% of snapshots — ~0.001 bps to cross. Not the constraint here.

fees

10 bps/side standard taker vs ~0.5 bps of conditional edge. The entire result. Even top VIP taker tiers lose.

latency

Edge decays on a ~1s timescale; +1,000 ms of delay erases it. Median feed latency on this setup: 64 ms.

fills

Simulated at displayed top-of-book quotes, no impact — fair for small size, optimistic beyond displayed depth.

adverse selection

The mirror image of the finding: makers filling these aggressive orders lose ~0.5 bps in expectation. The signal’s real value is quote skewing.

Fine print

Limitations

  • One afternoon, one venue, one regime: ~2.2 h usable data, BTC drifting mildly down — 45 of 47 test trades were shorts, so the long side is weakly tested.
  • Spot depth snapshots carry no exchange timestamp; the local receive clock is the analysis clock, and arrivals are bursty.
  • Several capture gaps (collection machine slept); labels are invalidated across gaps rather than interpolated.
  • Top-20 partial depth stream, not a reconstructed full book; overlapping labels mitigated with non-overlapping IC checks.
